# A practical evidence check for Fort Myers service-area pages

A local service page should do more than repeat a city name. Before publishing another Fort Myers page, check whether the page gives a customer enough evidence to understand the service, the coverage area, and the next step.

## 1. State the service-area boundary honestly

Say whether the business serves Fort Myers and nearby Southwest Florida communities. Do not imply a storefront, headquarters, or office that has not been verified. Clear service-area language is more useful than manufactured proximity.

## 2. Match the page to a real customer decision

The page should answer a specific question: what is offered, who it is for, what happens next, and how a person can make contact. A page assembled only around a location keyword rarely gives a visitor enough reason to act.

## 3. Check the complete inquiry path

Test the route from search result to page, form or call, confirmation, routing, and follow-up. A visible form is not evidence that an inquiry reaches the right person. Record the owner of each handoff and test it from a phone as well as a desktop.

## 4. Make local relevance inspectable

Useful local evidence can include accurate service boundaries, original observations, relevant photos, customer questions, and transparent research notes. Keep every claim tied to something a reader can inspect.

## 5. Fix the page before multiplying it

If the first local page has a weak offer, missing technical metadata, or a broken follow-up path, cloning it creates more maintenance without creating more value. Repair the underlying template and customer path first.

# Before you add another Fort Myers page, verify these five things

A service-area business can mention Fort Myers without pretending it has a storefront there. The useful question is whether the page helps a local customer understand the service, the coverage area, and the next step.

## 1. Confirm the business identity

Use the same public business name and canonical website everywhere. Publish an address only when it is real, customer-facing, and eligible under the listing provider's rules. If the business travels to customers, say that plainly.

## 2. Give the page a distinct job

A Fort Myers page should answer a local buyer's questions, not repeat a city name around generic service copy. Explain the service, who it is for, relevant coverage boundaries, what the process looks like, and what the visitor can do next.

## 3. Test the mobile customer path

Open the page on a real phone. Check whether the service and location are understandable, the main action is visible, phone or booking links work, forms expose clear labels and errors, and the confirmation state tells the visitor what happens next.

## 4. Check search interpretation

Verify the response status, canonical URL, title, main heading, internal links, structured data, sitemap inclusion, and robots rules. A page cannot support local discovery if it is duplicated, non-canonical, blocked, or disconnected from the rest of the site.

## 5. Record evidence before making claims

Separate what was observed from what was reported, unknown, or not applicable. A screenshot, HTML snippet, public profile, or measurement can support a finding. Rankings, calls, and conversion rates remain unknown without first-party records.

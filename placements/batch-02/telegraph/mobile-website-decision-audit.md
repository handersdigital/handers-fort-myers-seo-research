A desktop screenshot can make a local service website look complete while the phone experience quietly loses the inquiry. For many buyers, the mobile page is the actual sales conversation: they arrive with a problem, scan for fit, look for proof, and decide whether to call.

## Test the decision in one hand

I would begin with one important service page on a real phone-sized viewport. The first check is not decoration. It is whether a visitor can answer four questions without hunting:

1. What service is available?
2. Who is it for?
3. Does the business serve my area?
4. What should I do next?

The primary action should remain obvious without covering the content. Phone links need valid targets. Forms need labels, readable errors, a clear confirmation state, and enough space for touch input. Horizontal scrolling, tiny text, and unstable images are operating defects because they interrupt the decision.

## Keep proof close to the claim

Local pages often ask for action before they establish trust. A service claim should sit near the detail that supports it: a process, qualification, work example, policy, or useful explanation. The reader should not have to search the footer or a separate gallery to understand why the claim is credible.

Performance belongs in the same review. A fast score by itself is not the goal, but delayed content, moving controls, and late-loading forms change what a person can read and tap.

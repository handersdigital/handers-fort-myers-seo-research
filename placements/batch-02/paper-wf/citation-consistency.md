A directory listing is useful only when the business facts behind it can be verified and maintained. Publishing the same uncertain information in more places does not create trust; it makes the correction job larger.

## Establish the canonical facts

Before adding a citation, record the approved business name, website, phone, contact email, primary categories, hours, real locations, and service areas. Physical addresses and service areas are different facts. A company that travels to customers should not convert a market it serves into an office it does not operate.

The record should identify the source for each fact and the person responsible for changes. It should also list variations that are acceptable, such as a shortened display name, separately from errors that need correction.

## Inspect the destination before submission

Check whether the directory is relevant to the market or service, whether the page is public, whether the website field becomes a real link, and whether the operator can edit the listing later. Record login ownership and recovery details in the project credential system, not in the citation tracker.

After publication, verify the rendered page rather than trusting a success message. Confirm the visible facts, link target, link attributes, indexability, and edit path. Save the live URL and next review date.

## Correct first, expand second

If existing profiles disagree, fix the records the business controls before creating new ones. Accuracy and recoverability are better operating goals than a raw submission count.

Research the customer path, not just the ranking.

A listing can be visible while the website is unclear. A good page can still lose an inquiry if no one owns the next step. The method keeps those handoffs connected.

  1. 1

    Confirm the public identity

    Record the name, website, service area, contact path, and existing profiles. Do not infer an address or local presence.

  2. 2

    Test what a customer can do

    Review the service, proof, local context, main action, and confirmation state on a real phone and desktop browser.

  3. 3

    Separate evidence from interpretation

    Label every finding observed, reported, unknown, or not applicable before writing a recommendation.

The worksheet keeps the tested URL, device, date, reviewer, observation, evidence level, and next check together. That makes a recheck more useful than a one-time grade.

Local relevance needs a truth boundary.

Serving Fort Myers does not prove that a business has a Fort Myers office. This kit treats an address, Google Business Profile location, and proximity claim as facts that need verification.

Handers Digital uses service-area language. It does not claim a public Fort Myers storefront. The same rule belongs in every local audit: record what is confirmed, keep unknowns visible, and do not manufacture proximity as an SEO tactic.

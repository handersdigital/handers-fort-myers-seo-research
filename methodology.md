# Research methodology

## Purpose

This method tests whether a customer can find, understand, trust, and contact a
Fort Myers-area business online. It also records technical signals that help a
search engine interpret the website.

The method favors visible evidence. It does not infer sales, conversion rates,
search rankings, or company quality when those records are unavailable.

## Research questions

1. Is the business identity consistent and truthful?
2. Can a mobile visitor understand the service and service area quickly?
3. Is the main action visible and usable?
4. Can search engines crawl, index, and interpret the important pages?
5. Does the website show enough evidence for a visitor to trust the next step?
6. Can the team measure calls, forms, bookings, or other meaningful actions?

## Review unit

One record covers one public website on one date. Record the tested URL, device,
browser, network conditions, and reviewer. When a business has several
locations, keep each verified location separate.

## Sample selection for a market study

- Define the sectors before collecting websites.
- Record the source list and selection date.
- Use the same selection rule across sectors.
- Avoid choosing only strong or weak examples.
- Remove duplicates and parked domains.
- State the sample size and any exclusions.
- Do not claim the sample represents every Fort Myers business.

A useful starting set may include home services and construction,
healthcare and wellness, hospitality, professional services, retail, and
automotive or local services. The researcher should confirm the final sectors
before collection.

## Evidence levels

Use one of four labels for every item:

- **Observed:** directly visible or reproducible on the review date.
- **Reported:** supplied by the business or an authoritative source.
- **Unknown:** unavailable without account access or first-party records.
- **Not applicable:** irrelevant to this business or customer path.

Recommendations should cite the observation that prompted them. An API response,
browser screenshot, HTML snippet, public profile, or measurement export may be
used as evidence when it does not expose private data.

## Review sequence

### 1. Entity truth

Check the public name, website, service area, contact details, categories, and
existing profiles. Never add a location or phone number based on inference.

### 2. Customer understanding

Review the homepage and one important service page. Record whether the service,
audience, service area, proof, and next action are clear.

### 3. Contact path

Inspect calls, forms, booking links, and confirmation states. Do not submit a
test lead unless the business has authorized it.

### 4. Search interpretation

Review indexability, canonical URLs, titles, descriptions, headings, internal
links, structured data, sitemap coverage, and robots rules.

### 5. Mobile and accessibility basics

Check viewport behavior, readable type, tap targets, labels, landmarks, image
alternatives, keyboard access, and obvious contrast problems.

### 6. Measurement

Confirm whether meaningful actions can be measured. Mark analytics, call
tracking, CRM routing, or revenue attribution as unknown when account access is
unavailable.

## Publication rules

- Publish aggregate findings with a visible denominator.
- Keep negative examples anonymous without permission.
- Date screenshots and measurements.
- Distinguish a failed test from an unavailable test.
- State what the sample cannot establish.
- Offer a correction path.
- Never present a false local address as an SEO tactic.

## Recheck

Retest the original observation after a change ships. Record the new date and
evidence. A recommendation is complete only when the relevant behavior or
technical signal has been verified again.
